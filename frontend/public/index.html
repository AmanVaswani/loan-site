08Eligibility

What lenders actually look at.

Approval is never just about your credit score. These are the six variables that decide both your sanction and your rate.

01

Debt-to-income ratio

Total existing EMIs plus the new EMI should generally stay below 45–50% of net monthly income. Above that, most lenders decline regardless of score.

02

Credit history depth

Not just the score — lenders read repayment patterns, the number of open accounts, recent enquiries and any settlement or write-off flags.

03

Income stability and vintage

Length of employment or business vintage, consistency of inflows, and whether income has been rising, flat or volatile over the last 24 months.

04

Employer or business category

Category-A employers, government service and established businesses unlock better rates on identical profiles. We match your file to the right lender tier.

05

Age and residual working years

This sets maximum tenure — and tenure drives EMI affordability. For borrowers above 55, some lenders add endowment or insurance requirements.

06

Property or asset quality

For secured loans, clear title, approved plans and location determine the loan-to-value a lender will actually offer. We run a preliminary title check before you apply.

04Reading a Rate Sheet

The rate is never the whole price.

Five charges sit outside the headline rate. We itemise every one of them before you choose an offer.

01

Processing fee

Usually 0.5%–3% of the loan, deducted upfront. On a ₹20 lakh loan that's ₹10,000 to ₹60,000 that never reaches you but still accrues interest in your total cost.

02

Legal, valuation and technical charges

Common on home and secured loans. ₹8,000 to ₹45,000 depending on property value and location. Some lenders waive these — we always ask.

03

Insurance add-ons

Life cover bundled into a loan is often optional but sold as mandatory. Check whether term insurance outside the loan would be cheaper — it usually is.

04

Stamp duty and registration

Statutory and non-negotiable, but lender-borne varies. Clarify who pays before you sign, not after.

05

Penalty structure

Late-payment charges, bounce charges and foreclosure terms. On floating-rate loans to individuals, foreclosure charges are prohibited by RBI norms.

01Our Story

A conversation that needed to change.

In 2018, our founder sat in a bank branch watching a schoolteacher sign a loan agreement she didn't fully understand. She was being charged a processing fee the salesperson had described as "standard", an insurance premium she hadn't asked for, and an interest rate two points above what her own credit profile warranted.

None of it was illegal. All of it was unnecessary. And the gap between what she was offered and what she qualified for was, quite literally, a family's annual income over the loan's lifetime.

Loan ki Baat began as a spreadsheet — one person mapping every lender's real pricing against every customer's real profile, then telling people what they should actually be paying. That spreadsheet became a service. That service became this platform.

Our mission

To make borrowing transparent, so that every Indian who takes a loan does so knowing exactly what it costs and exactly what they qualified for.

Our measure

Not loans disbursed. Not revenue. We measure ourselves by the gap between what a customer was first offered and what they ultimately paid.

02What We Stand On

Four principles we won't trade.

They're boring, they're old-fashioned, and they're the reason customers refer their families to us.

Radical transparency

Every fee, every charge, every term — written down before you commit. If we can't put it on one page, it isn't clear enough.

Borrower-first, always

We will tell you not to take a loan. We've done it many times. Our incentive comes from lenders, but our duty is to you.

Data stewardship

Your documents are encrypted, stored in India, shared only with lenders you choose, and never sold to marketers. You can request deletion any time.

Plain language

No jargon, no euphemism, no "subject to terms". If a term is important, we explain it until you understand it — however long that takes.

Independent by design

No lender owns a stake in us. No lender gets preferential ranking. Commission structures are uniform across the panel.

Long-term accountability

We check in 30 days after disbursal and again at 12 months. Problems after the sale are still our problems.
